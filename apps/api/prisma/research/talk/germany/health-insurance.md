## Answered

**1. Is it compulsory, and for whom?**

**Yes, but there are different legal routes—not a single rule that automatically puts every resident into statutory insurance.**

- **Residence:** §193(3) VVG requires people with a **Wohnsitz in Germany** to maintain qualifying health insurance unless an exception applies, including statutory insurance or compulsory statutory membership. There are also exceptions for specified public healthcare entitlements, including asylum-benefit coverage. Holding a residence permit is not itself the trigger under this provision; residence is. **Not working does not remove the obligation.** [§193 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__193.html)
- **Employment:** §5(1)(1) SGB V generally makes paid employees compulsorily insured in the statutory system, subject to exclusions such as high earnings and marginal employment. [BMG: insured persons](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/gesetzlich-versicherte)
- **Students:** §5(1)(9) SGB V generally requires statutory insurance for students enrolled at state or state-recognised universities until age 30. Recognised reasons can extend this; international coverage rules, family insurance and exemptions can change the result.
- **Self-employed people:** Main-occupation self-employment generally does not create ordinary compulsory statutory membership. Special rules apply, including for qualifying artists, writers and farmers.
- **Non-working permit holders:** A permit alone does not establish statutory eligibility. The uninsured-person fallback in §5(1)(13) is restricted by §5(11): for third-country nationals, it requires the specified residence title and absence of a livelihood-security requirement. It is **not a universal entry route for newcomers**. [§5 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__5.html)

Consequently, somebody waiting to start a job still needs cover, but their future employee membership does not automatically cover those first weeks.

Cross-border rules can also leave a person insured through another country—for example, certain posted workers or students. German insurance is therefore not invariably the required solution. [EU official guidance](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/when-living-abroad/health-insurance-cover/index_en.htm)

**2. Statutory or private, and who gets to choose?**

For an ordinary employee, the **2026 general annual earnings threshold is €77,400 gross**, equivalent to €6,450 monthly. Regular annual remuneration must **exceed** it to escape compulsory employee GKV membership. This is different from the contribution-assessment ceiling. The historical threshold for qualifying people already privately insured on 31 December 2002 is €69,750. [BMG’s published 2026 figures](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/gesetzlich-versicherte), [§6 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__6.html)

Above the threshold, **private insurance is an option, not an obligation**:

- Existing GKV members can normally continue voluntarily.
- Someone starting their **first employment in Germany** above the threshold can join GKV voluntarily without the usual prior-insurance requirement. They must notify the fund within **three months after starting employment**. [§9(1)(3), (2)(3) SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__9.html)
- Self-employed and non-working newcomers cannot necessarily buy voluntary GKV membership simply by offering to pay. Eligibility must exist under the statutory rules. Qualifying family members can instead have contribution-free family insurance. If no statutory or recognised alternative route applies, the residence-based duty generally requires qualifying private cover. [§9 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__9.html), [§10 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__10.html), [§193 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__193.html)

**The choice is not freely reversible.** Returning from PKV to GKV generally requires a new legal entitlement or compulsory-insurance situation. A qualifying employee job can create one; simply preferring GKV cannot. After 55, §6(3a) adds substantial restrictions based on the preceding five years’ insurance and employment status—it is not an unconditional ban on everyone over 55. [BMG: switching systems](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/krankenversicherung/online-ratgeber-krankenversicherung/krankenversicherung/wechsel-zwischen-gkv-und-pkv), [§6 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__6.html)

Students can apply for exemption from student compulsory insurance within three months. **That exemption cannot be revoked**; it should not be presented as a private-insurance trial that can be cancelled to restore student GKV membership. [§8 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__8.html)

**3. What it costs in 2026**

For ordinary employee GKV contributions:

| Item | 2026 |
|---|---:|
| General contribution rate | 14.6% |
| Additional contribution | Fund-specific |
| Official average additional rate | 2.9% |
| Monthly contribution-assessment ceiling | €5,812.50 |
| Annual contribution-assessment ceiling | €69,750 |

Employer and employee normally split **both** the general and fund-specific additional contribution equally. At a 2.9% additional rate, that means **8.75% each**, up to the assessment ceiling. The 2.9% figure is not a guaranteed price at every fund.

Ordinary voluntarily insured self-employed people pay the whole contribution. Their **minimum monthly assessment base is €1,318.33**, even with lower income; assessable income can include investment and rental income.

| Self-employed GKV cover | Rate before additional contribution | Monthly minimum* | Monthly maximum* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Without sickness cash benefit | 14.0% | €222.80 | €982.31 |
| With sickness cash benefit from day 43 | 14.6% | €230.71 | €1,017.19 |

\*Using the 2.9% average additional rate; actual fund rates change these amounts. These are **health-insurance-only** figures. [BMG: 2026 contribution table](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/beitraege/seite)

**Care insurance is additional.** The standard social care-insurance rate is 3.6%; ordinarily 4.2% for childless members aged 23 or older, with specified exceptions. Discounts apply for additional qualifying children under 25. Self-employed members normally bear the full amount. At the minimum base, adding standard 3.6% care insurance to the €222.80 example produces approximately **€270.26 monthly**; with the childless surcharge, approximately **€278.17**. These totals are calculated from the published rates. [BMG: care-insurance financing](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/pflege/online-ratgeber-pflege/die-pflegeversicherung/finanzierung)

**4. The six-week gap before the first job**

Compulsory employee GKV membership begins **on entering the employment relationship**, not when the future contract is signed. An independent earlier entitlement—such as family insurance—must be assessed separately. [§186(1) SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__186.html)

For somebody relying on future employee GKV membership, the Foreign Office’s national-visa guidance expressly allows **incoming or travel insurance as the bridge**. Crucially:

- It must actually cover the interval until GKV starts.
- An exclusion for intended residence in Germany must not invalidate that initial coverage.
- The guidance permits termination when German statutory cover starts; otherwise, it says three months will regularly suffice for the expected transition.

This is official **visa-administration guidance**, not certification that every product labelled “incoming” meets every German insurance obligation. I could not verify a blanket equivalence between visa acceptance and compliance with §193 VVG. [Visa Handbook, “Lebensunterhalt bei nationalen Visa,” §2.4, September 2025, PDF pages 450–451](https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/blob/207816/e025d7a51aa0e20f5567c6f7478c8fd6/visumhandbuch-data.pdf)

**Back contributions depend on when membership legally began.** Where the uninsured-person fallback applies, §186(11) establishes a retrospective membership start; §256a expressly addresses contributions owed after late notification and provides for appropriate reduction and waiver of the associated late-payment surcharges. It does not provide a general cancellation of contributions. [§186 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__186.html), [§256a SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__256a.html)

Therefore, **“joining GKV later always creates a bill back to arrival” is wrong**. For the six-week example, the fund must first establish whether an earlier GKV membership existed. If it begins only with employment, that employment membership does not itself create six weeks of earlier GKV contributions.

Separately, delayed compliance with the private-insurance duty can trigger the **premium surcharge under §193(4) VVG**. That is a different mechanism from GKV arrears. [§193 VVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vvg_2008/__193.html)

**5. What the residence permit requires**

The legal chain is **§5(1)(1) AufenthG**—normally requiring a secured livelihood—and **§2(3) AufenthG**, which includes sufficient health-insurance protection. Statutory insurance expressly counts as sufficient. Exceptions to the livelihood requirement exist for particular residence categories, so this is not an absolute condition for every permit. [AufenthG §5](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/BJNR195010004.html), [§2(3)](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__2.html)

**The Ausländerbehörde does not universally demand GKV.** Qualifying private insurance can satisfy the requirement. Authorities assess its coverage and supporting evidence.

For an ordinary longer-term first permit, **do not assume travel insurance is enough because it was accepted for the visa**. Hamburg’s official guidance explicitly separates these stages: travel insurance may suffice in the entry procedure, but the first residence title issued inside Germany normally requires the fuller coverage described in its guidance. It also identifies purpose-specific exceptions, including Working Holiday arrangements. [Hamburg’s official leaflet](https://welcome.hamburg.de/resource/blob/17560/c987410a6f2d85efdd62e238305eac16/merkblatt-der-bis-zum-nachweis-kv-data.pdf)

Berlin likewise limits its general travel-insurance acceptance to entry and short stays of up to three months; private cover for longer residence is individually assessed. **“Incoming insurance is accepted for every first permit” is not verified and should not be published.** [Berlin immigration authority](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/faq/artikel.874993.php)

**6. What varies by Land or city**

**No regional variation in the core compulsory-insurance rules, the employee earnings threshold, or the statutory health-insurance contribution rates and assessment limits described above.** Those are federal rules.

There are, however, three distinct kinds of geographic difference:

- **Available insurers:** Federal law itself makes some fund choices depend on residence or workplace—for example, the local AOK and certain company or guild funds. Other choices are nationwide. Different fund-specific additional contributions are **insurer differences**, not Land-imposed contribution rates. [§173 SGB V](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgb_5/__173.html)
- **Published immigration-administration requirements:** Berlin’s December 2024 leaflet generally specifies a **€300 annual deductible**, allowing higher amounts after individual assessment. Hamburg’s guidance refers to a **€5,000 ceiling** and uses different evidence forms according to the intended duration. Hamburg also explicitly says a health-card copy alone is insufficient and asks for a current membership certificate. These belong under **local administrative guidance and procedure**, not different health-insurance statutes. I verified the published requirements, not how individual officers apply them. [Berlin leaflet](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/_assets/merkblatt_zur_krankenversicherung.pdf?ts=1734098796), [Hamburg guidance](https://welcome.hamburg.de/einreise-und-aufenthalt/hinweise-zur-aufenthaltserlaubnis/nachweis-ae-krankenversicherung-416918)
- **Related care insurance:** Saxony has a genuine geographically different employee/employer split prescribed within the federal system: **2.3% employee / 1.3% employer**, compared with **1.8% / 1.8%** elsewhere, before applicable child adjustments. This is **Pflegeversicherung**, not a different GKV health-insurance rate. [BMG](https://www.bundesgesundheitsministerium.de/themen/pflege/online-ratgeber-pflege/die-pflegeversicherung/finanzierung)

HEALTH INSURANCE IN GERMANY, WHICH IS NOT OPTIONAL

You must have it, and living here is what triggers it. Anyone with a residence in Germany must hold qualifying health insurance unless an exception applies. It is section 193(3) of the Insurance Contract Act, and it attaches to residing here, not to holding a permit and not to having a job. Not working does not excuse you.

Which system you are in is mostly decided for you. An employee is normally compulsorily in the statutory system. A student at a recognised university normally is too, until thirty. Someone self-employed as their main occupation normally is not. And a permit on its own does not get you into the statutory system: the fallback route for uninsured people is restricted for third country nationals and is not a general door for newcomers. Cross-border rules may also leave you insured by another country.

The threshold, and a three month window nobody mentions. An employee whose regular annual pay exceeds 77,400 euros in 2026, which is 6,450 a month, is outside compulsory statutory membership and may take private insurance. Private is an option there, not an obligation. If this is your first employment in Germany and you are over the threshold, you can still join the statutory system voluntarily without the usual prior insurance history, but you have to tell the fund within three months of starting work.

What it costs. The general statutory rate is 14.6 per cent, plus an additional contribution that each fund sets for itself; the official average for 2026 is 2.9 per cent. Employer and employee split both halves equally, so at the average that is 8.75 per cent each, up to a monthly assessment ceiling of 5,812.50 euros. A self employed person pays the whole thing on a minimum assessment base of 1,318.33 euros a month even if they earn less, which comes to about 222.80 a month without sickness cash benefit and about 230.71 with it, at the average additional rate.

Care insurance is on top and it is where Saxony differs. The standard rate is 3.6 per cent, or 4.2 for childless members aged twenty-three and over, with reductions for qualifying children. In Saxony the split between employee and employer is 2.3 and 1.3 per cent, where everywhere else it is 1.8 and 1.8. That is a real geographic difference written into the federal system, and it is care insurance rather than health insurance.

The weeks before your job starts. Statutory membership through employment begins when the employment begins, not when you sign the contract. For the gap, the Foreign Office's visa guidance accepts incoming or travel insurance as a bridge, provided it actually covers the interval, is not invalidated by an exclusion for intending to live in Germany, and can be ended when statutory cover starts.

But do not carry that assumption into the permit office. Travel insurance accepted for your visa is not automatically enough for the first residence permit issued inside Germany. Hamburg's guidance separates those two stages explicitly and asks for fuller cover for the permit. Berlin and Hamburg also differ in what they will accept: Berlin's leaflet generally wants an annual deductible of no more than 300 euros, with more allowed after an individual assessment, while Hamburg refers to a 5,000 euro ceiling and says a copy of your health card is not enough on its own, it wants a current membership certificate.

Being late is not automatically a bill back to your arrival. Whether you owe back contributions depends on when your membership legally began. What is the same everywhere. The duty, the earnings threshold, the statutory rates and the assessment limits are federal and identical wherever you live. What differs is which funds you can join, since some depend on where you live or work, what each fund charges as its additional contribution, what your city's immigration office will accept as evidence, and the Saxon care insurance split.

# Health insurance in Germany, which is not optional

**Agreed 2026-09-12**, after two turns in `talk/germany/health-insurance.md`.
**Eleven** overclaims found at sign-off, the most of any case. Every sentence
below is the corrected one.

## What a reader is told

**You must have it, and living here is what triggers it.** Anyone with a
residence in Germany must hold qualifying health insurance unless an exception
applies. It is **§193(3) VVG**, and it attaches to residing here, not to holding
a permit and not to having a job. Not working does not excuse you.

**Which system you are in is mostly decided for you.** An employee is normally
compulsorily in the statutory system (§5(1)(1) SGB V). A student at a recognised
university normally is too, until thirty (§5(1)(9)). Someone self-employed as
their main occupation normally is not. A permit on its own does not get you into
the statutory system: the uninsured-person fallback in §5(1)(13) is restricted by
**§5(11)** for third-country nationals and is not a general door for newcomers.
Cross-border rules may also leave you insured by another country.

**The threshold, and a three month window.** The general employee threshold is
**€77,400** in 2026, equivalent to **€6,450** a month. Someone starting
employment above it is generally outside compulsory employee membership. Someone
**crossing it during an existing compulsory membership normally leaves only at
year-end**, provided their pay also exceeds the following year's threshold.
Private insurance is an option above the threshold, not an obligation. If this is
your **first employment in Germany** and you are over the threshold, you can join
the statutory system voluntarily without the usual prior-insurance history, but
you must tell the fund **within three months** of starting work (§9(1)(3)).

**What it costs.** The general statutory rate is **14.6%**, plus an additional
contribution each fund sets for itself; the official 2026 average is **2.9%**.
Employer and employee normally split the general and additional contributions
equally, giving **8.75% each** at the official average, up to the monthly
assessment ceiling of **€5,812.50**. Special contribution-sharing rules apply,
including for midijobs.

An ordinarily voluntarily insured **self-employed** GKV member pays the whole
contribution, assessed on at least **€1,318.33** monthly even with lower income.
At the average additional rate the monthly minimum is about **€222.80** without
sickness cash benefit or **€230.71** with it; higher assessable income means
higher contributions, up to the ceiling.

**Care insurance is on top, and it is where Saxony differs.** The standard social
care-insurance rate is **3.6%**. Childless members normally pay **4.2%** from the
month after their twenty-third birthday, subject to statutory exceptions.
Discounts apply for the second through fifth qualifying children under
twenty-five.

**For employment located in Saxony**, the standard split is **2.3% employee and
1.3% employer**; elsewhere it is 1.8 each. These figures are before child-related
adjustments and special rules such as midijobs. **The geographic trigger is the
place of employment, not where the employee lives** (§58 SGB XI).

**The weeks before your job starts.** Statutory membership through employment
begins **when the employment begins** (§186(1)), not when you sign the contract.
For the gap, the Foreign Office's visa guidance accepts incoming or travel
insurance as a bridge, provided it actually covers the interval, is not
invalidated by an exclusion for intending to live in Germany, and can be ended
when statutory cover starts. That is visa-administration guidance; it is not
certification that such a product satisfies §193 VVG.

**Do not carry that assumption into the permit office.** Travel insurance
accepted for your visa is not automatically enough for the **first residence
permit issued inside Germany**. Hamburg's guidance separates those two stages
explicitly. Berlin and Hamburg also differ in what they accept: Berlin's leaflet
generally wants an annual deductible of no more than **€300**, with more allowed
after individual assessment; Hamburg refers to a **€5,000** ceiling and says a
copy of your health card is not enough on its own, it wants a current membership
certificate.

**Being late is not automatically a bill back to your arrival.** Whether you owe
back contributions depends on when your membership legally began; if it begins
with your employment, that does not create contributions for the weeks before it.
Where the uninsured-person route applies, late notification can produce
contributions owed from the legal membership start; **§256a** provides for
appropriately reducing those back contributions and **fully waiving** the
associated late-payment surcharges, subject to its implementing rules. For the
private-insurance duty, **§193(4) VVG** provides a separate premium surcharge
when the application to conclude the required contract is made **more than one
month** after that duty arose.

**What is the same everywhere.** The duty, the earnings threshold, the statutory
rates and the assessment limits are federal and identical wherever you live. What
differs is which funds you can join, since some depend on where you live or work
(§173 SGB V); what each fund charges as its additional contribution; what your
city's immigration office accepts as evidence; and the Saxon care-insurance
split.

## What this means for the schema, and it is a third shape

Until this case, every regional difference found was a **region attaching its own
charge to a national duty**: Hamburg's €16, Istanbul's and Antalya's chamber
tariffs. Saxony is not that. It is a **federal rule that itself carries a
regional variant**, written into federal law.

And the trigger is **where the employment is, not where the reader lives**. A
region dimension keyed only on residence would return the wrong contribution for
somebody living in Brandenburg and working in Dresden.

So a rule's region needs to say **what kind of connection it is**: where you
live, where you work, or where the transaction happens. SB-168 should not assume
residence.
