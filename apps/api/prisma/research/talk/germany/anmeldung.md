## Answered

1. **The federal rule**

**Anmeldung is a federal duty administered locally. The ordinary deadline has no regional variation.** Under **§17(1) BMG**, anyone moving into a dwelling must register with the Meldebehörde **within two weeks after moving in**. Nationality and EU citizenship do not determine whether this duty applies. For children under 16, registration falls to the person into whose dwelling they move: §17(3). [§17 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__17.html)

There is **no universal three-month grace period for newcomers**. The relevant exceptions are:

- **Already registered in Germany:** moving into another dwelling for a stay intended to last no more than six months requires no additional registration. If the stay continues beyond six months, register within the following two weeks.
- **Otherwise living abroad and not registered in Germany:** §27(2) provides the temporary-stay exception, with registration required within two weeks after three months. Someone relocating their home to Germany should not be described as automatically qualifying.
- These temporary-dwelling exceptions do **not** apply to asylum seekers and other foreigners temporarily entering assigned reception accommodation: §27(3). [§27 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__27.html)

**Hotels have a separate rule:** §29(1) brings stays exceeding six months into ordinary registration; someone without a registered German dwelling must register within two weeks once the hotel stay exceeds three months. Foreign guests also have a separate arrival-day guest-registration obligation, subject to statutory exceptions and electronic alternatives. That hotel procedure is not municipal Anmeldung. [§29 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__29.html)

Federal administrative guidance additionally says intended use lasting under two weeks generally does not constitute moving into a dwelling. This is qualified guidance, not an absolute exemption. There are also narrow diplomatic/treaty exemptions. [BMGVwV 17.1.1](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_27092022_VII2201041418.htm), [§26 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__26.html)

2. **What the Länder may change**

**The two-week deadline is fixed federally.** The BMG gives Länder no power to replace it with a different ordinary registration deadline. **I found no Land with a different statutory deadline.**

**The late-registration offence and maximum fine are also federal:** intentional or negligent failure to register correctly and on time can attract **up to €1,000**, under §54(2)(1), (3). This is a ceiling, not a mandatory €1,000 charge. Different enforcement decisions or amounts imposed are not different statutory ceilings. I found no Land with a different maximum for this offence. [§54 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__54.html)

But **“Länder have no registration legislation” would be wrong**. §1 lets Land law designate registration authorities. §55 permits supplementary rules on additional data, central registers, forms and specified data transfers. For example, **Bavaria’s BayGMPP Article 1 designates municipalities; Article 7 establishes the central register operated by AKDB**. These are legal provisions, not merely office habits. They do not alter the ordinary registration deadline. [§1 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__1.html), [§55 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__55.html), [BayGMPP](https://www.gesetze-bayern.de/Content/Document/BayAGBMG/true)

One additional difference needs a verification flag: **Hamburg publishes a €16 registration fee** for arrivals from abroad, citing its *MeldeGebO*; Berlin and Munich publish registration as free. I verified those service-page statements, but **could not retrieve the operative Hamburg fee ordinance**, so I cannot independently confirm its tariff provision. Do not store this as merely discretionary office behaviour—or as a verified statutory tariff yet. [Hamburg](https://www.hamburg.de/service/info/111142065/n0/), [Berlin](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/120686/), [Munich](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/info/wohnsitzanmeldung/1063475/n0/)

For storage: **national substantive duty and exceptions; supplementary Land legislation where evidenced; local procedures separately.**

[Berlin procedure](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/120686/), [Munich procedure](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/info/wohnsitzanmeldung/1063475/n0/), [Hamburg procedure](https://www.hamburg.de/service/info/111142065/n0/)

Hamburg’s blanket attendance wording sits uneasily with federal guidance permitting representation. **I have not verified a Hamburg law overriding that guidance**; store the published requirement with that unresolved qualification. [BMGVwV 23.0](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_27092022_VII2201041418.htm)

**Appointments and waiting:** Munich releases additional appointments before opening, in the morning for the following week, and in the afternoon for the following day. Berlin reported achieving its aggregate 14-day appointment target in March 2026. Neither establishes an individual’s current waiting time; **I did not verify live availability**. [Munich appointments](https://stadt.muenchen.de/infos/terminvereinbarung-buergerbueros.html), [Berlin’s dated report](https://www.berlin.de/rbmskzl/aktuelles/pressemitteilungen/2026/pressemitteilung.1671216.php)

**Post:** I verified no ordinary postal Anmeldung route in these three cities’ registration pages. Berlin and Munich publish online/in-person routes; Hamburg publishes personal attendance for arrivals from abroad. Do not confuse posting a missing document with completing registration by post.

4. **The Wohnungsgeberbestätigung**

**Federally required, with no regional variation in the required information:** under §19, the housing provider or their authorised person must confirm the move within two weeks, in writing or electronically to the authority. It must contain:

- Housing provider’s name and address.
- Owner’s name if different.
- Actual move-in date.
- Dwelling’s address.
- Names of the people subject to registration.

[§19 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__19.html)

“Landlord confirmation” is an imperfect translation. **The provider is whoever actually supplies the accommodation.** For a subtenant, that is the main tenant; this also covers accommodation provided free. Thus, in a shared flat or family home, the relevant owner/main tenant signs—or authorises someone—not automatically any flatmate or relative. [BMGVwV 19.1](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_27092022_VII2201041418.htm)

Intentional or negligent refusal, incorrect confirmation or delay can attract **up to €1,000**, under **§54(2)(3), (3)**. The **€50,000** maximum concerns offering a fictitious registration address, not ordinary refusal or delay. [§54 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__54.html)

5. **What Anmeldung unlocks**

- **Tax ID: normally automatic.** First registration triggers allocation and notification of the Steueridentifikationsnummer, if one has not already been allocated. It remains valid for life. If no letter arrives within three months, BZSt provides a request procedure. [BZSt](https://online.portal.bzst.de/SharedDocs/Leistungsbeschreibung/DE/erneute_mitteilung_der_ID-Nr.html)
- **Bank account: separate application, never automatic.** Anmeldung is **not a universal legal prerequisite**: BaFin expressly includes people without a fixed residence among those potentially entitled to a *Basiskonto*. That does not establish the documentation policy of every ordinary current-account product. [BaFin](https://kontenvergleich.bafin.de/de/glossar/basiskonto-nach-dem-zkg)
- **Health insurance: arrange separately; Anmeldung does not enrol you.** The appropriate route depends on employment and other circumstances. Employees choose a statutory insurer where applicable, and employers handle social-insurance registration; other groups must arrange the relevant coverage themselves. Official newcomer guidance calls for coverage from entry, so publishing “wait for Anmeldung to obtain insurance” would be wrong. [Federal newcomer guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/de/leben-in-deutschland/geld-versicherung/krankenversicherung), [Employer guidance](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/de/unternehmen/integrieren/unterstuetzen)

These are not Land-specific rewards for registration.

6. **The situation that could embarrass the product**

**Missing confirmation:** §19(2) requires the resident to notify the Meldebehörde **without delay** if the provider refuses or the confirmation is otherwise late. [§19 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__19.html)

Federal administrative guidance goes further: **registration must proceed if the person actually moved in, even without the confirmation; it must be supplied afterwards.** A lease does not replace the confirmation. This is nationwide guidance, not a Munich concession. [BMGVwV 19.0 and 23.0.1.1](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_27092022_VII2201041418.htm)

Munich makes the process concrete: submit its missing-confirmation declaration, supply the confirmation within the next two weeks, and contact the office if refusal or delay continues. Its form warns that supplying **neither confirmation nor an explanation** can lead to fine and coercive-payment proceedings. [Munich’s form](https://stadt.muenchen.de/dms/Home/Stadtverwaltung/Kreisverwaltungsreferat/fachspezifisch/HA-II/Buergerbuero/Dokumente/Erklaerung_fehlende_Wohnungsgeberbestaetigung.pdf)

**Temporary accommodation:** that label alone does not prevent registration. §20 defines a dwelling broadly as an enclosed space used for living or sleeping; apply the dwelling/hotel exceptions in point 1. Federal guidance says restrictions on lawful occupation do not remove the registration duty. [§20 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__20.html), [BMGVwV 17.1.2](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_27092022_VII2201041418.htm)

For someone with **no actual dwelling**, I have not verified a nationwide substitute-address procedure. I would not promise that a friend’s mailbox or correspondence address can serve as Anmeldung.

## Answered

**1. Hamburg: €16 for the qualifying family together.**

I found stronger evidence in Hamburg’s official gazette, available through its publisher without using the broken law portal.

The original ordinance’s tariff item 2 covers:

> “Verarbeitung der Anmeldung einer Person oder einer Familie mit gleicher Wegzugs- und Zuzugsadresse”

That makes **one person or one qualifying family** the charging unit. Notice the condition: the family shares **both the previous and the new address**, not merely the destination. [2015 official gazette, pp. 273–274](https://www.luewu.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GVBL_HH_2015-42.pdf).

The amendment dated 2 December 2025 sets **tariff item 2 at €16**, effective 1 January 2026. Hamburg’s current service wording also explicitly says €16 per person **or family group with the same moving details**. Together, these resolve the per-person/per-family ambiguity: **a qualifying family of four pays €16 total.** [2025 official gazette, pp. 779 and 784](https://www.luewu.de/gvbl/ausgabe-nr-43-vom-16-12-2025-seiten-723-786-groesse-48-mb/), [current Hamburg service page](https://www.hamburg.de/service/info/111112974/).

I have not verified a complete definition of which relationships qualify as a “family” for this tariff. Do not extend this finding to unrelated housemates or relatives arriving from different addresses.

**3. The authority is Hamburg’s general fee legislation.**

[Original ordinance](https://www.luewu.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GVBL_HH_2015-42.pdf), [2025 amendment](https://www.luewu.de/gvbl/ausgabe-nr-43-vom-16-12-2025-seiten-723-786-groesse-48-mb/).

Thus the fee’s enabling legislation is **Land administrative-fee law**. The BMG supplies the registration duty being processed. The gazette establishes this directly; I could not retrieve the current consolidated text of Hamburg’s general Fee Act to verify its provisions individually.

**For SkipBureau: store the federal registration duty and the Land fee rule separately.** Hamburg’s charge is a legally prescribed regional rule, not merely an office practice. The two-week duty has no regional variation; the fee does.

**4. Publishable deadline sentence.**

**A person moving permanently from abroad to Germany and taking up a flat must register within two weeks of moving into that flat under §17(1) BMG—not within three months.** [§17 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__17.html).

- **A temporary stay by someone who otherwise lives abroad and is not registered in Germany:** §27(2), sentence 3. If the stay continues beyond three months, registration becomes due within the following two weeks. The exclusions in §27(3) still apply. [§27 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__27.html).
- **Accommodation in a hotel or another qualifying commercial accommodation establishment by someone without a registered German dwelling:** §29(1), sentence 2 requires registration within two weeks once the stay exceeds three months. §29(4) contains a corresponding rule for specified camping/boat accommodation. The separate guest-registration requirement is a different obligation. [§29 BMG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bmg/__29.html).

The rule, and it is the same everywhere. Anyone who moves into a dwelling must register with the Meldebehörde within two weeks of moving in. It is section 17(1) of the Bundesmeldegesetz, it is federal, and no Land can lengthen or shorten it. Your nationality does not matter and neither does being an EU citizen. For a child under sixteen, the person whose dwelling they move into registers them.

Two weeks, not three months. If you are moving to Germany to live and you take a flat, you have two weeks from moving in. The three-month figure is real but it describes other situations: a temporary stay by somebody who otherwise lives abroad and is not registered in Germany, where registration falls due within two weeks after the stay passes three months; and a stay in a hotel or similar by somebody with no registered German dwelling, on the same pattern. Asylum seekers and others in assigned reception accommodation are outside those exceptions.

If you miss it. Failing to register on time, deliberately or carelessly, can be fined up to a thousand euros. That is a ceiling and not a standard charge. The fifty thousand euro figure you may read about is for offering somebody a fictitious registration address and has nothing to do with being late.

The landlord confirmation, and what to do without one. The person who provides your accommodation, which for a subtenant is the main tenant rather than the owner, must confirm your move in writing or electronically within two weeks. The confirmation must carry their name and address, the owner's name if that is somebody else, the date you actually moved in, the address, and the names of everyone registering. If they refuse or are late you must tell the registration office without delay, and federal guidance says the registration must go ahead anyway if you really did move in, with the confirmation supplied afterwards. A lease is not a substitute for it. A provider who refuses, delays or confirms falsely can also be fined up to a thousand euros.

What it costs, and this is where Germany differs from itself. Registration is free in Berlin, Munich, Düsseldorf, Wiesbaden, Freiburg and under Saxony's state guidance. Hamburg charges sixteen euros, set by its own fee ordinance and raised to that figure on 1 January 2026. The charge is for one person or for a family who share both the address they left and the address they are arriving at, so a qualifying family pays sixteen euros once, not each. We could not establish which relationships count as a family for that tariff, so do not assume it covers housemates or relatives arriving separately. Hamburg's fee comes from Hamburg's general fee legislation rather than from the federal registration law, which is why it can exist at all while the two-week deadline cannot vary.

What registering gets you. Your tax identification number arrives on its own after your first registration, and it is yours for life; if no letter comes within three months the Federal Central Tax Office has a procedure for asking again. A bank account is a separate application and, as a matter of law, registration is not a precondition: the financial regulator expressly includes people with no fixed residence among those who may be entitled to a basic account. Health insurance is also separate and registering does not enrol you in anything. Official guidance for newcomers expects you to be covered from the day you arrive, so do not wait for your Anmeldung to arrange it.

Cities differ in how, not in what. Berlin registers by appointment, in person or through an authorised representative, and its online route is for moves within Germany rather than a first arrival from abroad. Munich also allows a representative and publishes a form for a missing landlord confirmation. Hamburg's page for arrivals from abroad says everyone moving in must attend in person, which sits awkwardly with federal guidance allowing representation, and we could not find a Hamburg rule that overrides it. # Registering where you live in Germany

**Agreed 2026-09-12**, after three turns in `talk/germany/anmeldung.md`. The
draft put up for sign-off had **six** overclaims. Each sentence below is the
corrected one, and none of it may be softened, sharpened or given colour when it
becomes a guide.

## What a reader is told

**The rule, and it is the same everywhere.** Unless a statutory exemption or
exception applies, anyone who moves into a dwelling must register with the
Meldebehörde **within two weeks**[^bmg-17-two-weeks] of moving in. It is **§17(1)
Bundesmeldegesetz**, it is federal, and no Land can lengthen or shorten it. Your
nationality does not matter and neither does being an EU citizen. For a child
under sixteen[^bmg-17-under-sixteen], the person whose dwelling they move into registers them.

**Two weeks, not three months.**[^bmg-17-two-weeks] If you are moving to Germany to live and you
take a flat, you have two weeks[^bmg-17-two-weeks] from moving in. The three-month figure[^bmg-27-three-months] is real
but it describes other situations: a temporary stay by somebody who otherwise
lives abroad and is not registered in Germany, where registration falls due
within two weeks[^bmg-27-two-weeks] after the stay passes three months[^bmg-27-three-months] (**§27(2)**); and a stay in
a hotel or similar by somebody with no registered German dwelling, on the same
pattern (**§29(1)**). The temporary-stay exception under §27(2) does not apply
to asylum seekers or other foreign nationals who move temporarily into a
reception facility or other assigned accommodation (**§27(3)**).

**If you miss it.** Failing to register on time, deliberately or carelessly, can
be fined **up to €1,000**[^bmg-54-fine-late-registration] (§54(2)(1), (3)). That is a ceiling, not a standard
charge. The **€50,000**[^bmg-54-fine-fictitious-address] figure you may read about is for offering somebody a
**fictitious registration address** and has nothing to do with being late.

**The landlord confirmation, and what to do without one.** The person who
provides your accommodation, which for a subtenant is the main tenant rather
than the owner, must confirm your move within two weeks[^bmg-19-confirmation-deadline][^bmg-17-two-weeks][^berlin-landlord-confirmation-two-weeks], either in a signed
written confirmation or electronically **directly to the Meldebehörde through
the procedure under §19(4)**. It must carry their name and address, the owner's
name if that is somebody else, the date you actually moved in, the address, and
the names of everyone registering.

If they refuse or are late you must tell the registration office **without
delay**[^bmg-19-without-delay] (§19(2)), and federal guidance says the registration **must go ahead
anyway** if you really did move in, with the confirmation supplied afterwards[^bmgvwv-confirmation-afterwards]. A
lease is not a substitute for it. A provider who refuses, delays or confirms
falsely can also be fined up to €1,000[^bmg-54-fine-confirmation].

**What it costs, and this is where Germany differs from itself.** Registration
is free in Berlin[^berlin-registration-free], Munich[^munich-registration-free], Düsseldorf[^duesseldorf-registration-free], Wiesbaden[^wiesbaden-registration-free], Freiburg[^freiburg-registration-free] and under Saxony's
state guidance[^saxony-registration-free]. **Hamburg charges €16**[^hh-registration-fee-16], set by its own fee ordinance and raised
to that figure on 1 January 2026[^hh-fee-amendment-2026]. The charge is for one person **or for a family
who share both the address they left and the address they are arriving at**[^hh-tariff-2015-item-2], so

# Registering where you live in Germany

**Agreed 2026-09-12**, after three turns in `talk/germany/anmeldung.md`. The
draft put up for sign-off had **six** overclaims. Each sentence below is the
corrected one, and none of it may be softened, sharpened or given colour when it
becomes a guide.

## What a reader is told

**The rule, and it is the same everywhere.** Unless a statutory exemption or
exception applies, anyone who moves into a dwelling must register with the
Meldebehörde **within two weeks**[^bmg-17-two-weeks] of moving in. It is **§17(1)
Bundesmeldegesetz**, it is federal, and no Land can lengthen or shorten it. Your
nationality does not matter and neither does being an EU citizen. For a child
under sixteen[^bmg-17-under-sixteen], the person whose dwelling they move into registers them.

**Two weeks, not three months.**[^bmg-17-two-weeks] If you are moving to Germany to live and you
take a flat, you have two weeks[^bmg-17-two-weeks] from moving in. The three-month figure[^bmg-27-three-months] is real
but it describes other situations: a temporary stay by somebody who otherwise
lives abroad and is not registered in Germany, where registration falls due
within two weeks[^bmg-27-two-weeks] after the stay passes three months[^bmg-27-three-months] (**§27(2)**); and a stay in
a hotel or similar by somebody with no registered German dwelling, on the same
pattern (**§29(1)**). The temporary-stay exception under §27(2) does not apply
to asylum seekers or other foreign nationals who move temporarily into a
reception facility or other assigned accommodation (**§27(3)**).

**If you miss it.** Failing to register on time, deliberately or carelessly, can
be fined **up to €1,000**[^bmg-54-fine-late-registration] (§54(2)(1), (3)). That is a ceiling, not a standard
charge. The **€50,000**[^bmg-54-fine-fictitious-address][^bmg-19-6-fictitious-address] figure you may read about is for offering somebody a
**fictitious registration address** and has nothing to do with being late.

**The landlord confirmation, and what to do without one.** The person who
provides your accommodation, which for a subtenant is the main tenant rather
than the owner, must confirm your move within two weeks[^bmg-19-confirmation-deadline][^bmg-17-two-weeks][^berlin-landlord-confirmation-two-weeks], either in a signed
written confirmation or electronically **directly to the Meldebehörde through
the procedure under §19(4)**. It must carry their name and address, the owner's
name if that is somebody else, the date you actually moved in, the address, and
the names of everyone registering.

If they refuse or are late you must tell the registration office **without
delay**[^bmg-19-without-delay] (§19(2)), and federal guidance says the registration **must go ahead
anyway** if you really did move in, with the confirmation supplied afterwards[^bmgvwv-confirmation-afterwards]. A
lease is not a substitute for it. A provider who refuses, delays or confirms
falsely can also be fined up to €1,000[^bmg-54-fine-confirmation].

**What it costs, and this is where Germany differs from itself.** Registration
is free in Berlin[^berlin-registration-free], Munich[^munich-registration-free], Düsseldorf[^duesseldorf-registration-free], Wiesbaden[^wiesbaden-registration-free], Freiburg[^freiburg-registration-free] and under Saxony's
state guidance[^saxony-registration-free]. **Hamburg charges €16**[^hh-registration-fee-16], set by its own fee ordinance and raised
to that figure on 1 January 2026[^hh-fee-amendment-2026]. The charge is for one person **or for a family
who share both the address they left and the address they are arriving at**[^hh-tariff-2015-item-2], so
a qualifying family pays €16 once[^hh-registration-fee-16], not each. We could not establish which
relationships count as a family for that tariff, so do not assume it covers
housemates or relatives arriving separately. Hamburg's fee comes from Hamburg's
**general fee legislation** rather than from the federal registration law, which
is why it can exist at all while the two-week deadline[^bmg-17-two-weeks] cannot vary.

**What registering gets you.** Your **tax identification number** arrives on its
own after your first registration[^bzst-id-at-first-registration] and is yours for life[^bzst-for-life]; if no letter comes
within three months[^bzst-three-months] the Federal Central Tax Office has a procedure for asking
again. A **bank account** is a separate application and, as a matter of law,
registration is not a precondition: the financial regulator expressly includes
people with no fixed residence among those who may be entitled to a basic
account. **Health insurance** is also separate and registering does not enrol you
in health insurance. Official guidance for newcomers expects you to be covered
from the day you arrive[^miig-cover-from-arrival], so do not wait for your Anmeldung to arrange it.

**Cities differ in their registration procedures.** Berlin registers by
appointment, in person or through an authorised representative, and its online
route is for moves within Germany rather than a first arrival from abroad.
Munich also allows a representative and publishes a form for a missing landlord
confirmation. Hamburg's page for arrivals from abroad says everyone moving in
must attend in person, which sits awkwardly with federal guidance allowing
representation, and we could not find a Hamburg rule that overrides it. The
service pages we checked for Berlin, Munich and Hamburg do not offer ordinary
registration by post.
