Context you can rely on and need not re-derive, from the Anmeldung case in another conversation: the Anmeldung is a federal duty with a two-week deadline that no Land may vary, Hamburg charges sixteen euros for it under its own fee ordinance while several other cities charge nothing, and municipalities differ in procedure. ## Answered

1. **Where the power sits.** The competent **Ausländerbehörde decides the application and issues the Aufenthaltserlaubnis**. In Berlin this is the Landesamt für Einwanderung (LEA); in Munich, the city’s Servicestelle für Zuwanderung und Einbürgerung. The Bundesagentur für Arbeit may need to consent to employment; it does not issue the residence permit. [§71 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__71.html), [§18 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18.html).

   Länder responsibilities extend beyond choosing the authority: they administer federal law and regulate administrative organization and procedure within the constitutional framework. Berlin also publishes **Verfahrenshinweise zum Aufenthalt in Berlin (VAB)**, expressly described as interpretations of legal terms and guidelines for exercising discretion. That is an administrative-policy layer, distinct from both federal eligibility law and an individual office’s habits. [Articles 83–84 GG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gg/art_84.html), [Berlin’s explanation of the VAB](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/service/downloads/artikel.875097.php).

   **Verification gap:** I verified the VAB’s existence and stated purpose, but could not reliably read the current PDF, listed as dated 31 August 2026. I therefore cannot yet give you a verified, currently applicable employment-permit instruction, its precise binding effect, and what outcome it changes. Naming the VAB alone does not complete that part of your question.

2. **The fee.** **Federal, with no Land-by-Land standard fee variation:** the ordinary first employment Aufenthaltserlaubnis issued as an electronic residence card costs **€100**, whether its validity is up to one year or longer. This is published in **§45(1)(a)–(b) Aufenthaltsverordnung**, a federal regulation. [§45 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__45.html).

   €100 is the standard, not a universal charge for every applicant or document format. Federal exemptions and reductions exist. For example, exceptional issuance under §78a as a sticker reduces the fee by €44, explaining Berlin’s published €56 option; that is **not a separate Berlin tariff**. [§45b AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__45b.html), [§52 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__52.html).

3. **What happens while you wait.** **The following distinctions are federal: no regional variation in these legal effects.**

   | Situation when a timely application is made | Stay pending the decision | Work | Travel and return |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Valid national **D visa** | Previous title continues under **§81(4)**. | Existing work permission and restrictions continue. Applying does not itself authorize a different job. | A valid **§81(4) Fiktionsbescheinigung**, with a valid passport, permits travel and re-entry. |
   | Lawful **visa-free** stay without a residence title | Stay is deemed lawful under **§81(3), sentence 1**. | The application alone does **not** authorize employment. | A **§81(3) Fiktionsbescheinigung does not authorize re-entry**. |

   These are explained explicitly in the [official Fiktionsbescheinigung guidance](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/326233/).

   **§81(5)** requires issuance of the document called a **Fiktionsbescheinigung**. The qualifying application triggers the statutory protection; the certificate evidences it. A further work rule matters: under **§81(5a)**, once issuance of the qualifying employment/education title has been initiated, its specified employment is permitted while the card is being produced; that permission must appear in the certificate. This is later than merely submitting an application. [§81 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__81.html).

   Visa-free tourist admission is not automatically permission to move to Germany and obtain an employment permit domestically. **§41 AufenthV** provides that route for specified nationalities, with an application deadline of 90 days. A short-stay **C visa** is also excluded from §81(4)’s continuation rule. [§41 AufenthV](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthv/__41.html), [§81 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__81.html).

4. **The appointment problem.** **Yes, official acknowledgment exists.** Berlin says some departments have appointments available only months ahead. Its official emergency route uses the responsible department’s contact form; documented emergencies can receive a Fiktionsbescheinigung by dispatch or an early appointment. Published grounds include urgent travel within four weeks, threatened job loss, or threatened benefit termination. [Berlin appointment guidance](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/termine/termin-vereinbaren/).

   **The protective route is to submit the application before expiry, without waiting for the appointment.** Munich expressly says no appointment is needed to apply. Its online submission produces a downloadable confirmation for authorities and employers. [Munich FAQ](https://stadt.muenchen.de/infos/sze-faq.html).

   Berlin’s employment route likewise takes the application online before allocating an appointment. Save its confirmation. Berlin separately recognizes certain timely contact-form appointment requests as evidence of continued stay, but **that local guidance should not become a nationwide claim that any appointment request protects you**. For this employment application, use the published dedicated application route. [Berlin employment application](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/329328/), [Berlin appointment guidance](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/termine/termin-vereinbaren/).

   Munich also has an emergency appointment service: threatened job/benefit loss or urgent travel within seven days. Starting employment or changing employer is expressly not an emergency under that service. These access criteria are **local procedure**, not different statutory residence rights. [Munich emergency service](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/info/servicestelle-fur-zuwanderung-und-einburgerung/10339065/).

5. **What changes in practice.** Comparing the **same permit: §18b, skilled employment with an academic qualification**:

   | Published procedure | Berlin LEA | Munich SZE |
   |---|---|---|
   | Application | Dedicated online application; payment before submission. | Online **or by post**. |
   | Appointment | Allocated following a positive assessment; originals brought to appointment. | Allocated after application review. |
   | Published time | **4–6 weeks for electronic-card availability** following the issuance stage. No numerical total application-processing estimate on this page. | **Up to seven months** processing; high application numbers and staffing shortages expressly acknowledged. |
   | Concrete checklist difference | Explicit housing-size and current housing-cost evidence, including lease and rent evidence for tenants. | Explicit additional *Versicherung zur Ausübung der Beschäftigung* for first applications and certified German/English translation of a foreign degree. |

   Sources: [Berlin’s §18b service page](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/329328/), [Munich’s corresponding service page](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/info/servicestelle-fur-zuwanderung-und-einburgerung/10278359/).

   These establish **differences in published procedure and evidence checklists**, not different substantive eligibility laws. An item absent from one checklist is not proof the authority never requests it. Both allow further document requests. In particular, Munich’s separate employment declaration implements an assurance required federally by **§18(2)(4a)**; it is not evidence of a Bavaria-only eligibility condition. [§18 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__18.html).

6. **The thing you would get wrong.** I could not verify an official ranking establishing the *single most common* misunderstanding.

   The mistake I would prioritize is: **“I applied, so I can stay, start work and travel while waiting.”** Those are three separate questions. A timely application can protect residence while granting neither new work permission nor re-entry rights. The decisive checks are the previous status, **§81(3) versus §81(4)**, and any expressly documented employment permission under **§81(5a)**. An application receipt should not be presented as interchangeable with a travel-valid Fiktionsbescheinigung. [§81 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__81.html), [official certificate guidance](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/326233/).

---

GETTING A RESIDENCE PERMIT IN GERMANY

Who decides. The Ausländerbehörde where you live decides your application and issues the permit: in Berlin the Landesamt für Einwanderung, in Munich the city's Servicestelle für Zuwanderung und Einbürgerung. The employment agency may have to consent to your job, but it does not issue the permit.

What it costs, and this one is federal. A first residence permit for employment, issued as an electronic card, costs a hundred euros, whether it runs for a year or longer. It is set in the federal Aufenthaltsverordnung, not by your city. If you see fifty-six euros quoted in Berlin, that is the federal reduction for the exceptional sticker format, not a Berlin price. Exemptions and reductions exist.

What happens while you wait, and this is the part to read twice. Applying in time protects your stay. It does not automatically let you work, and it does not automatically let you travel. Which of those you get depends on how you were in Germany when you applied.

If you held a national D visa, your previous permission continues while the decision is pending, under section 81(4). Your existing permission to work continues with the restrictions it already had, and applying does not let you switch to a different job. With a valid 81(4) Fiktionsbescheinigung and a valid passport, you can travel and come back.

If you were here lawfully without a residence title, visa-free, your stay counts as lawful under section 81(3), but the application by itself does not let you work, and an 81(3) Fiktionsbescheinigung does not let you re-enter Germany. Leaving may mean you cannot get back in.

Two more things about working. Under section 81(5a), once the authority has started issuing your employment permit, the specified work is allowed while the card is being produced, and that permission has to be written on your certificate. And arriving visa-free as a tourist is not a general route to applying for a work permit inside Germany: section 41 of the Aufenthaltsverordnung opens that route only to specified nationalities, with a ninety day deadline, and a short-stay C visa does not get the continuation rule at all.

If you cannot get an appointment. This is real and officially acknowledged: Berlin says some departments have nothing available for months. There is an official route. Use the responsible department's contact form and document why it is urgent. Berlin publishes the grounds it accepts, which include travel within four weeks, a job at risk and benefits at risk, and it can send a Fiktionsbescheinigung out or bring your appointment forward.

Cities do the same thing differently. For the same permit, skilled employment with a degree: Berlin takes the application online only and takes payment before you submit, allocates an appointment after a positive assessment, and asks for evidence of your housing size and what your rent costs. Munich accepts the application online or by post, allocates an appointment after review, and asks for a declaration about the employment and a certified translation of a foreign degree. Berlin publishes four to six weeks for the card once issuance starts, and gives no total estimate. Munich publishes up to seven months and says plainly that it has too many applications and not enough staff. These are differences in procedure and in what each office lists, not different eligibility rules, and neither list is a promise that nothing else will be asked for.

The mistake to avoid. It is believing that applying settles all three questions at once. Staying, working and travelling are separate, and which you have depends on your previous status, on whether you are under 81(3) or 81(4), and on whether employment is expressly written on your certificate. An application receipt is not the same document as a Fiktionsbescheinigung that is valid for travel.

## Answered

- **“Applying in time protects your stay.”**  
  Replace with: “Applying before your national D visa or residence permit expires normally preserves that title under §81(4); applying during a lawful visa-free stay without a residence title protects your stay under §81(3). A short-stay C visa does not receive this protection. These rules apply nationwide.” The original is too broad. [§81 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__81.html)

- **“Use the responsible department's contact form and document why it is urgent.”**  
  Replace with: “In Berlin, submit the employment-permit application through the dedicated online application before your current permission expires; you do not need to wait for an appointment. For additional help in a documented emergency, use the responsible department’s contact form.” The original presents Berlin’s emergency channel as the general solution to obtaining an appointment. [Berlin application procedure](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/329328/), [Berlin appointment guidance](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/termine/termin-vereinbaren/)

- **“Berlin publishes the grounds it accepts, which include travel within four weeks, a job at risk and benefits at risk, and it can send a Fiktionsbescheinigung out or bring your appointment forward.”**  
  Replace with: “Berlin assesses emergency requests for urgent travel within the next four weeks evidenced by a booking, or threatened job loss or benefit termination because of missing valid documentation, evidenced by an employer’s or Jobcenter/Sozialamt’s letter. If it accepts the emergency, it can send a Fiktionsbescheinigung or offer a prompt appointment.” Ordinary planned travel or a job at risk for unrelated reasons is broader than the published criteria. [Berlin emergency criteria](https://www.berlin.de/einwanderung/termine/termin-vereinbaren/)

- **“Berlin publishes four to six weeks for the card once issuance starts, and gives no total estimate.”**  
  Replace with: “Berlin’s service page for this permit publishes four to six weeks for the electronic card after the issuance stage; that page gives no numerical estimate for the whole application process.” We verified the page, not the absence of an estimate throughout Berlin’s publications. [Berlin service page](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/329328/)

- **“Staying, working and travelling are separate, and which you have depends on your previous status, on whether you are under 81(3) or 81(4), and on whether employment is expressly written on your certificate.”**  
  Replace with: “Staying, working and travelling are separate. Existing work permission continues under §81(4); new work permission under §81(5a) arises when issuance of the qualifying title is initiated and must be recorded on the certificate. Re-entry requires the appropriate valid travel documents.” The certificate records the work permission; its wording alone does not create or determine the statutory right. [§81 AufenthG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/aufenthg_2004/__81.html), [official certificate guidance](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/326233/)
