# SB-234, Research which nationalities §41 AufenthV lets apply inside Germany, and what §81(3) protects

**Exit:** on the deployed API, for the skilled worker's residence permit, a visa-free reader of a nationality
§41(1) AufenthV names, other than the United Kingdom, whose §41 class a nationality code cannot identify, is
told the ninety days to apply in Germany; a visa-free reader of another nationality, §41(2)'s six included, is
not told that route; and a visa-free reader is told what applying protects under §81(3); each fact naming its
official page. Each of those readers has said their nationality; a visa-free reader who has not is asked for
it, because the ninety days depend on it. This is the card's exit as narrowed after the first and fourth checks.

**The exit is narrowed, on the card, to what this permit and a nationality code can say** (the second and
fourth checks). §41(2)'s six nationalities are named by §41 too, but only for a stay without employment beyond
§17(2) AufenthV, so their route never reaches a skilled worker's permit and they are not told the ninety days
for it. And §41(1) names the United Kingdom only in the Withdrawal Agreement's sense, which the product's
nationality `gb` cannot tell from other kinds of British nationality. So the exit names the nationalities of
§41(1) other than the United Kingdom. If the research finds Article 2(d) covers every kind of British
nationality, `gb` joins the group and the exit is widened back; otherwise a card is filed for telling a British
citizen the ninety days, which needs a detail the product does not have. The notes still name British
nationals among §41(1)'s, so no British reader is told the route is not theirs.

**With it, SB-241's exit** (a child of SB-225, from its roast): a reader on a Schengen C visa is told for the
skilled worker's permit that applying does not keep their visa valid until the decision, the fact naming §81
AufenthG, and a D visa holder is still told that it does. It needs the same session, the same statute page and
the same case file, so it is researched and published with this card, moved to in progress when its version is
written, and closed on its own exit and its own live query.

**SB-242 first.** The publish's read-back asks each version as a reader built from its criteria, and gives a
version with no nationality no nationality, so the second version below would be asked for one and reported
missing. SB-242 fixes that reader; this card's publish waits for it.

## Why

A reader of a nationality §41 names can apply for a residence title inside Germany within ninety days of
arriving visa-free, and other readers cannot use that route, so without the list the product cannot tell them
apart; and a visa-free reader is not told that applying protects their stay while it does not let them work or
come back in. SB-225 published Germany's residence permit for a skilled worker with a degree from
`agreed/germany/residence-permit.md`, whose definitions do not carry §41's nationalities or §81(3). Its roast
found that a C visa holder, given no answer, is never warned that applying does not preserve their stay.

## What the pages say, read 2026-09-15 to ask exact questions

Read from the raw pages, so the conversation can be asked exactly; what the agreed document says still comes
from the conversation, as the owner's order of 2026-09-12 requires.

- **§41(1) AufenthV**: nationals of Australia, Israel, Japan, Canada, the Republic of Korea, New Zealand,
  the United Kingdom "im Sinne des § 1 Absatz 2 Nummer 6 des Freizügigkeitsgesetzes/EU" and the United
  States may enter and stay visa-free for more than a short stay, and may obtain a required residence title
  in Germany.
- **§1(2) Nr. 6 FreizügG/EU**: British nationals are "die in Artikel 2 Buchstabe d des Austrittsabkommens
  genannten Personen", the Withdrawal Agreement's definition of a United Kingdom national.
- **§41(2)**: the same for nationals of Andorra, Brazil, El Salvador, Honduras, Monaco and San Marino "die
  keine Erwerbstätigkeit mit Ausnahme der in § 17 Abs. 2 genannten Tätigkeiten ausüben wollen". That is §17(2)
  of the AufenthV itself, since the same page names the Aufenthaltsgesetz and the Freizügigkeitsgesetz/EU
  wherever it means them: activities that under §30 Nr. 2 and 3 BeschV do not count as employment, for up to
  90 days in twelve months. A skilled worker's permit is for employment, so §41(2)'s route does not reach it.
- **§41(3)**: the title is to be applied for within 90 days of entry, ending early on expulsion or a time
  limit under §12(4) AufenthG. **§41(4)**: none of this applies to an ICT card.
- **§39 Nr. 3 AufenthV**: a national of a state in Annex II of Regulation (EU) 2018/1806 who is lawfully in
  Germany, or who holds a valid Schengen visa, may obtain a residence title in Germany if the conditions of an
  entitlement to it arose after entry, unless the entitlement is under §§16b, 16e or 19e. Whether they did is
  a question of the reader's history, such as when their job offer came, which no detail the product asks can
  answer.
- **§5(2) AufenthG**: a residence permit normally requires entry with the required visa; sentence 2 lets the
  authority waive that where the conditions of an entitlement are met, and requires it where catching up the
  visa procedure is unreasonable. **§5(3)'s last sentence** waives §5(2) for §§18a, 18b and 19c(2) only "in
  Anwendung von § 10 Absatz 3 Satz 5", a foreigner who withdrew an asylum application and entered before
  29 March 2023.
- **§81(2) AufenthG**: a title that may be obtained after entry is to be applied for "unverzüglich nach der
  Einreise oder innerhalb der in der Rechtsverordnung bestimmten Frist". **§81(3)**: a foreigner lawfully in
  Germany without a residence title who applies for one has their stay count as permitted until the decision;
  applied for late, only their deportation is suspended.
- **§81(4) sentence 2**: "Dies gilt nicht für ein Visum nach § 6 Absatz 1", and **§6(1)** names two visas, the
  Schengen visa (Nr. 1) and the airport transit visa (Nr. 2). **§4(1) Nr. 1** makes the Schengen visa a
  residence title, so its holder is not "ohne einen Aufenthaltstitel" and §81(3) does not reach them either.
  Sentence 3 lets the authority order a late application's continuation to avoid undue hardship; whether it
  reaches a Schengen visa, which sentence 2 excludes, is asked.
- **The Federal Administrative Court, 1 C 22.18 of 19 November 2019**,
  <https://www.bverwg.de/de/191119U1C22.18.0>, headnotes: §81(3) and (4) "stehen in einem sich ausschließenden
  Alternativverhältnis", and Schengen visas, those of other Schengen states included, are residence titles "für
  die die Fortgeltungsfiktion nach § 81 Abs. 4 Satz 2 AufenthG ausgeschlossen ist". Its press text: a Schengen
  visa holder who applies in time finds that "gilt das Visum weder fiktiv fort noch gilt sein Aufenthalt bis zur
  Entscheidung der Ausländerbehörde weiterhin als erlaubt". It does not decide sentence 3.
- **§81(5a)**: in the cases of (3) and (4), the employment the future title describes counts as permitted
  from when its issue is initiated, recorded on the certificate.
- **Berlin's Fiktionsbescheinigung page**, <https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/326233/>: for a reader
  lawfully without a visa because of their nationality, the certificate is issued under §81(3), "Diese
  Fiktionsbescheinigung berechtigt hingegen nicht zur Wiedereinreise in das Bundesgebiet. Auch die Aufnahme
  einer Erwerbstätigkeit ist damit nicht gestattet."; and "Für Inhaber eines Schengen-Visums für kurzfristige
  Aufenthalte (Kategorie C) kann keine Fiktionsbescheinigung ausgestellt werden." A Land's page, so it can
  carry notes and a Berlin fact, not a federal fact.

## Publishing, and the specs around it

SB-225's resolver test walks every status any version of the permit names and expects each to be told the
federal facts in a Land no rule names and to be asked where in a Land holding a named place. A visa-free reader
would be asked their nationality and a Schengen visa holder told their facts anywhere, so both would fail it,
and its last line expects a Schengen visa holder to get no answer; the publish runs that spec as a check. So
first, in its own commit, that test walks only the statuses some version names a place for, and its last line
reads Germany's file: a Schengen visa holder is told the facts of the version for `de.schengen-visa` where the
file has one, and nothing where it has none. Both pass before the data and after it.

Then, with SB-242 done, the case is published with
`npm run research:publish -w @skipbureau/api -- src/rules/research/germany/residence-permit.ts`, which commits
the case file with the agreed document, the transcript and `sessions.json`; its reset line is run when it
reports. Then, in its own commit, a resolver test reading its expectations from the file: a visa-free reader
of each member told the first version's facts, a visa-free reader of `br` (a §41(2) nationality), of `in` (in
no list) and of `gb` told the second's without the ninety days, a visa-free reader who has not said their
nationality asked, and a Schengen visa holder told the third's. Planted: the ninety days put on the second
version, which the test must fail, and the third version removed, which SB-225's assertion must then read as no
answer.
